Featured Work

Distinguishability threshold for random geometric graphs

With Zach Hunter and Benny Sudakov

arXiv PDF

The spherical random geometric graph (RGG) with parameters \(n\), \(d\) and \(p\) is defined by choosing \(n\) uniformly random points on the unit sphere in \(\mathbb{R}^d\) and connecting any two that are sufficiently close, where the distance threshold is chosen so that the probability of forming an edge is \(p\).

The main question about these graphs is the following:
For which values of \(d\) is this model distinguishable from the Erdős–Rényi binomial random graph of the same density?

This question was posed by Bubeck, Ding, Eldan and Rácz in 2016. They observed that triangles are slightly more likely to occur in the RGG than in the binomial random graph, and proposed a test that distinguishes between the two distributions whenever \(d\ll n^3p^3(\log(1/p))^3\).

Moreover, they conjectured that when \(d\gg n^3p^3(\log(1/p))^3\), it is asymptotically impossible to distinguish between the RGG and binomial random graph distributions, since their total variation distance tends to zero.

In this paper, we prove their conjecture whenever \(p\gg n^{-1/5}\), by establishing a precise upper bound on the probability that any fixed graph \(H\) is obtained under the RGG model.

From small eigenvalues to large cuts, and Chowla's cosine problem

With Zhihan Jin, István Tomon and Shengtong Zhang

arXiv PDF Quanta

We show that there exists an absolute constant \(\gamma > 0\) such that for every \(A \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_{>0}\) we have \[ \min_{x \in [0,2\pi]} \sum_{a \in A} \cos(ax) \leq -\Omega(|A|^\gamma). \] This gives the first polynomial bound for Chowla's cosine problem from 1965. To show this, we prove structural statements about graphs whose smallest eigenvalue is small in absolute value. As another application, we show that any graph \(G\) with \(m\) edges and no clique of size \(m^{1/2-\delta}\) has a cut of size at least \(m/2 + m^{1/2+\varepsilon}\) for some \(\varepsilon = \varepsilon(\delta) > 0\). This proves a weak version of a celebrated conjecture of Alon, Bollobás, Krivelevich, and Sudakov. Our proofs are based on novel spectral and linear algebraic techniques, involving subspace compressions and Hadamard products of matrices.
